**User's Question:** under the hood math of pytorch from the basics

**Answer:**
Based on the provided website content, the "under the hood" mathematics and mechanics of PyTorch operate on several foundational concepts:

1. **N-Dimensional Tensors and Memory Layout (ATen & Strides):**
   At the mathematical and structural core of PyTorch is the tensor, an n-dimensional data structure consisting of metadata (such as data type, device location, and **strides**). Strides are fundamental for translating multi-dimensional logical tensor positions into physical memory addresses, allowing efficient mathematical slicing and view creation without duplicating data. Furthermore, PyTorch separates the tensor interface from physical memory storage, utilizing C++ (via the ATen tensor library) for foundational tensor operations and kernel execution.

2. **Define-by-Run Dynamic Computational Graphs:**
   PyTorch uses a "define-by-run" paradigm where computational graphs are built dynamically as operations are executed. When tensors have `requires_grad=True`, operations track these computations to dynamically construct a computational graph node by node.

3. **Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation (Autograd):**
   Under the hood, PyTorch implements reverse-mode automatic differentiation through its `autograd` package. Every node in the computational graph tracks operations through gradient functions. When `backward()` is called, PyTorch automatically computes partial derivatives and gradients by applying the chain rule backward through the dynamic graph, mapping variables to their respective autograd metadata.

4. **Optimization and Parameter Updates:**
   Once gradients are computed via backpropagation, optimization algorithms (such as SGD, RMSprop, and Adam from the `torch.optim` package) abstract the underlying mathematical updates to the network parameters. This involves zeroing out accumulated gradients (`optimizer.zero_grad()`) and executing step updates (`optimizer.step()`) to adjust weights according to the computed loss derivatives.
